I study how people adapt to stress and adversity, and I build measurement systems that can establish what humans, and increasingly AI systems, can actually do.

My work sits at the intersection of psychological science, psychometrics, and computation:

  • Computational measurement. Can a model learn to perform psychometric inference across instruments and populations without being refitted for every new dataset? Through METER I am building simulation-trained models that transfer to unseen instruments, and mapping where that transfer breaks down.
  • Training and assessment infrastructure. Through Weftwell I am building AI-simulated interactions for training and assessing interpersonal and helping skills, and asking whether performance in simulation reflects transferable human competence.
  • AI evaluation and model psychology. I bring construct validity, measurement invariance, and causal experimental design to questions about what AI systems represent and how robust their behavior is under social pressure, conflicting authority, and psychologically challenging contexts.

The question that connects these threads: when a system, human or artificial, appears to reason, understand, or respond appropriately, what evidence would establish the capabilities underlying that behavior?

About Me

I am a psychological and population scientist with expertise in clinical psychology, epidemiology, and biostatistics. I am an Adjunct Professor at Teachers College, Columbia University, and a researcher affiliated with UNSW Sydney. I am the co-founder and principal investigator of METER, a research program on pretrained psychometric inference, and the founder of Weftwell, which builds AI training and assessment infrastructure for interpersonal and helping skills. Before this I served as Psychosocial Wellbeing Advisor with the United Nations. Over more than 16 years I have led research and implementation projects on trauma, stress, psychological adaptation, and social epidemiology across academic and humanitarian settings, including population studies and trials in Timor-Leste, Papua New Guinea, Malaysia, Bangladesh, and Australia, and I have served as principal investigator on research funded by Australia's National Health and Medical Research Council. I have authored or coauthored more than 100 peer-reviewed publications, including the WHO Problem Management Plus intervention, the Integrative ADAPT Therapy randomized trial, and the Adaptive Stress Index.
